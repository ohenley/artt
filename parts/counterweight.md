# Counterweight

[Find your part](README.md) · [Part index](reproduction.md) · [CAD drawings](../cad/README.md) · [Things to confirm](../TO-CONFIRM.md#counterweight-and-rest)

## What it is

The weight at the back of the arm that balances the arm, shell and cartridge; sliding it along the arm's rear stub and locking it with a set screw sets the stylus force (Factory, [XA owner's manual, scan p. 5](../docs/archive/vinyl-nirvana/ar-turntable-history/ar-xa-manual.md)). Photo wanted: an original XA weight beside a rule.

**Measure every sample the same way.** The owner holds an XA, an XB, an AR77XB and a spare brass weight from another XA. For each one:

1. Weigh it with its set screw, then without, on a scale that reads to 0.1 g (CW1, CW2, CW22).
2. Calipers: outside diameter at three places, length along the bore, bore diameter at both ends (CW3, CW4, CW5).
3. Thread gauge on the set screw; calipers from one end face to the locking hole (CW8, CW9).
4. Look through the bore: does it run through, and is it centred? Measure the wall around it (CW6).
5. Photograph both end faces and the side with a rule in the frame, and write the model and serial on the record.

**The XA and XB part numbers match in pattern.** The January 1979 XA price list gives counterweight 1400092-0 and set screw 1501008-0; the XB assembly gives counterweight 400092 and set screw 501008. The same pattern holds for the belt (1400134-0 against 400134) and the cartridge shell (1404003-0 against 404003), and does not hold for the pulley (1400067-0 against 400126). Whether the two counterweights are the same part in size and mass is Unconfirmed, CW12. The 1969 service manual used the older numbers 952 and 942.

## If you make or change it

**Keep:**

- **The mass.** The part is defined by its mass, not its outline. Only one XA value exists, 98.8 g with the set screw. A range needs several measured originals; until then the single value stands and the range is Unconfirmed, CW22. Do not invent a tolerance.
- **The bore.** 9.5 mm reported. That is within 0.03 mm of ⅜ in, and AR dimensioned in inches, so the drawing enters ⅜ in (9.525 mm) as derived. A fit on the arm stub is not established: the stub diameter is CW17.
- **Inside the space available.** The largest diameter and length that clear the arm rest, the top plate and the closed cover, and the usable length of the stub, are not recorded: CW16, CW17. A longer or fatter part can hit any of them.

**Free:** edge chamfers, surface finish, and where the locking screw sits around the circumference.

**What else is affected:**

- **The range of shell plus cartridge the arm can balance.** A lighter weight balances less at the same travel. AR supplied an extra section for some cartridges (sheet L-1591), which is the factory's own answer to a weight that was too light.
- **The arm's inertia.** Villchur writes that the inertial mass of the counterweight must be added to, not subtracted from, the arm's total inertial mass, and that the arm should be as light as possible for stability (Factory, [Villchur 1962, Part 1, p. 22](../docs/originals/villchur-1962-part-1.pdf)). A lighter material needs a bigger part for the same mass, and a bigger part sits with its centre further from the pivot.
- **The stylus-force setting.** About ¹⁄₃₂ in of counterweight movement changes the stylus force by ¼ gram, and tightening the set screw can move it that much (Factory, XA owner's manual, scan p. 5). The balance formula is stylus force = counterweight mass × (distance moved past the balance point) / (pivot-to-stylus length). The pivot-to-stylus length is 9 in (Factory, Villchur 1962, Part 1, p. 69). The distance from the pivot to the counterweight at both ends of its travel is not recorded, CW18, so the formula cannot yet give the balance range.
- **The case that fails.** Put the numbers in: 98.8 g × 0.794 mm / 228.6 mm = 0.34 g per ¹⁄₃₂ in. The manual says ¼ g. The two do not agree. Either the manual's figure is rounded, or the weight on that table was lighter than 98.8 g, or the lever is not that simple. Logged as CW19; it is one more reason to weigh several originals before trusting one number.
- **The [tonearm rest](tonearm-rest.md)** and the cover: a bigger weight can touch them, CW16.

## In another material

The formula: length = mass / (density × π/4 × (outside diameter² − bore²)), then add the length that puts back the metal removed by the locking hole. The four inputs: target mass (CW1), density (CW15, from the archived material pages), bore (CW5), outside diameter (CW3). The drawing computes the length (CW14) and prints the formula.

1. Pick the largest outside diameter that clears the arm rest, the top plate and the cover (CW16).
2. Compute the length from the formula, or rebuild the drawing with your numbers.
3. Weigh the finished part and trim the length until it is inside the range. The scale is the final check, whatever the drawing says.

Worked example, target 98.8 g, 28 mm diameter, 9.5 mm bore, no locking hole. The annular area is 544.9 mm². Every number recomputed for this page.

| Material | Density used, with its source | Length needed | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brass | 8.5 g/cm³. Brass is 8.4 to 8.73 g/cm³ (Standard, [Wikipedia, Brass](../docs/archive/wikipedia/brass.md)), which gives 20.8 to 21.6 mm | 21.3 mm | Works. 0.7 mm shorter than the reported 22 mm: the set screw is inside the 98.8 g, and the locking hole adds about 0.05 mm |
| Stainless steel | 7.9 g/cm³ (Standard, [Wikipedia, SAE 304 stainless steel](../docs/archive/wikipedia/sae-304-stainless-steel.md)) | 23.0 mm | Works if the arm stub has 1 mm to spare, CW17 |
| Aluminium | 2.70 g/cm³ (Standard, [Wikipedia, 6061 aluminium alloy](../docs/archive/wikipedia/6061-aluminium-alloy.md)) | 67.2 mm | Fails at this diameter: three times the length |
| Aluminium, 40 mm diameter | 2.70 g/cm³ | 30.9 mm | Works only if 40 mm clears the arm rest and the cover: Unconfirmed, CW16 |

The reverse case: an aluminium part with the brass one's outline, 28 mm by 22 mm, weighs about 32 g, one third of the brass weight. So the AR77XB's aluminium counterweight is either a bigger part or a much lighter one that balances less. Which it is: CW13, to be measured on the owner's AR77XB.

## Check after fitting

1. Weigh the finished part with its set screw. It must be inside the range on this page.
2. Slide it onto the stub and lock it. It must not rock, and the set screw must bite without bottoming on the bore.
3. With the shell and cartridge fitted, balance the arm so it floats level, then set the stylus force the cartridge needs with a gauge. The weight must reach that force with thread and stub to spare at both ends.
4. Swing the arm across the record and back to its rest, then close the cover. The weight must touch nothing.
5. Play the inner grooves of a record: the arm must track them without skipping.
