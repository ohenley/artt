# What the designer and the factory stated and measured

[The evidence](README.md) · [Period tests](period-tests.md) · [Owner tests](owner-tests.md) · [Against the reference tables](compared.md) · [Where the AR is weaker](weaker.md) · [Bench method](bench-method.md)

Tier 1 of the evidence: the figures Edgar Villchur published in 1962 and the figures Acoustic Research printed on its own paper. Every value here is a Factory value. A guaranteed limit is marked as a limit; a production test is marked as a test. Nothing on this page is an independent measurement: those are on the [period tests](period-tests.md) and [owner tests](owner-tests.md) pages.

## The design papers, 1962

Edgar Villchur, A New Turntable-Arm Design, *Audio*, September 1962 ([Part 1](../originals/villchur-1962-part-1.pdf)) and October 1962 ([Part 2](../originals/villchur-1962-part-2.pdf)). Page numbers are the printed ones.

### Arm and shell

| Item | Stated | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cartridge shell mass | The first prototype's aluminium shell was too heavy. An acrylic plastic shell cut the mass by more than half, to 7 g, and so also cut the counterweight mass needed | Part 1, p. 22 |
| Vertical pivot height | ¼ in above the record surface. Warp wow under the conditions he describes is about 0.05 percent | Part 1, p. 22 |
| Balance | Neutral static balance, so the stylus force does not change with the height of the arm. The rear section slopes 1.5 degrees so that neutral balance holds at any counterweight setting | Part 1, p. 22 |
| Arm resonance | In the 10 cps region with typical stereo cartridges. The drive motor's fundamental is 6⅔ cps (400 rpm), and the two must not fall close together | Part 1, p. 22 |
| McProud tracing test | A 45 rpm record is placed against the spindle, off centre, and the arm swung back and forth. Conceived for stylus forces of 6 to 8 g. The AR arm begins to fail it between ½ and ¾ g, depending on the cartridge mass | Part 1, p. 23 |
| Damping | The arm is damped when dropped and free while playing, with ⅛ in of free vertical play at the stylus | Part 1, p. 23, Fig. 6 |
| Effective length | 9 in, pivot to stylus | Part 1, p. 69 |
| Overhang | 0.688 in, set with a plastic template over the spindle. Factory-set for a cartridge with ⅜ in between mounting centres and stylus tip; the arm tube slides to suit others | Part 1, p. 69 |
| Tracking error | Maximum tracking-error index 0.32 degrees per inch of groove radius, calculated for radii of 5.7 in outside and 2.4 in inside. Maximum error 1.8 degrees, at the outermost groove | Part 1, p. 69 |

### Suspension

| Item | Stated | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mounting resonance | 3.5 cps, chosen so that the frequencies of floor vibration and acoustic feedback lie well above it | Part 1, pp. 20–21 |
| What is suspended | The platter bearing and the arm pivot on one rigid frame, hung on springs, isolated from the motor board and the outside | Part 1, p. 20 |

### Drive, speed, flutter, rumble

| Item | Stated | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Platter | Total weight 3.3 lb | Part 2, p. 36 |
| Motors | A permanent-magnet synchronous drive motor, 18-pole, 400 rpm, about 150 in-oz running torque, with a 600 rpm hysteresis starting motor adding about 30 in-oz. A little over 5 in-oz at the platter | Part 2, p. 36 |
| Speed under load, the test | From 2½ g of stylus force, weight is added to the pickup on the outer grooves until the speed changes 0.3 percent, which is 21 lines a minute on a standard stroboscope card. A U.S. penny adds 3 g, a nickel 5 g | Part 2, p. 36 |
| Scale of those loads | Inner-groove drag exceeds outer-groove drag by about 2 g of equivalent stylus force. A Dust Bug equals about 6 g. A heavily recorded passage, a gram or two | Part 2, p. 36 |
| Five tables compared | Tables 1, 2, and the AR stayed inside 0.3 percent with 5 g added. Tables 4 and 5 fell outside it with 3 g added. Table 1: heavy-duty hysteresis motor, very heavy platter. Table 2: light motor and platter. Table 4: heavy motor and platter. Table 5: lightweight. The AR he classes as medium weight. The full result is in [the comparison page](compared.md#a-speed-under-added-load-one-method-one-tester) | Part 2, p. 36 |
| His conclusion | Neither the "heavy" or "light" class nor the force needed to stop the platter by hand tells the useful torque. Table 4 takes some effort to stop by hand and still has less useful torque than the AR or table 2 | Part 2, pp. 36, 38 |
| Speed drop under load | The AR slows by several tenths of a percent under enough added load, entirely through belt slip; the motor itself stays synchronous | Part 2, p. 38 |
| Speed tolerance | Platter and pulley diameters are held so that the speed from one table to the next is always within 0.16 percent | Part 2, p. 38 |
| Flutter | Conforms to the 1953 NAB Standards of Good Engineering Practice for broadcast equipment, which are in raw flutter. Production testing at AR uses a weighted flutter and rumble meter, DataService Corp. model FM-3, because a raw percentage says little about what is heard | Part 2, p. 38 |
| Rumble | Meets the NAB rumble standard. Production testing uses the weighted meter above | Part 2, p. 38 |

Villchur's own warning stands over every comparison on these pages: the statement that one table has 0.1 percent flutter and another 0.2 percent does not reveal which has the better flutter performance, because the rate of the flutter matters as much as its amount (Part 2, p. 38).

## The specification sheet

[Technical Specifications of the AR Turntable](../archive/vinyl-nirvana/ar-turntable-history/1969-ar-xa-technical-specifications.md), AR Inc., Cambridge, Massachusetts, citing the NAB standard of March 1964. Every figure is a guaranteed limit, "as a condition of sale", not a measurement of any one table.

| Item | Guaranteed limit | Method named on the sheet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rumble | At least 35 dB below 1 cm/sec in the plane of modulation at 100 cps, each channel | NAB: meter ballistics and playback equalisation as specified. The sheet says the 35 dB figure applied to both lateral and vertical rumble is "a very exacting standard" and that many published rumble figures 20 dB better than it come from not following the NAB conditions |
| Wow and flutter | 0.1 percent maximum, combined | NAB, "when measured as specified". The sheet says lower figures are easy to reach by changing the meter's ballistic characteristics |
| Speed | ±0.3 percent, which is 21 lines a minute on a 216-line stroboscope card | Every table is tested to hold this with an extra 5 g of stylus force, equivalent to a dust bug |
| Weighted indices | AR also tests to its own weighted flutter and rumble indices and does not publish them, because no standard exists to compare them with | Stated on the sheet |

The 1962 paper and the sheet agree: the published numbers are NAB limits, and the factory's own pass or fail test is a weighted one that it kept to itself.

## The brochure

[AR Inc. turntable brochure](../archive/vinyl-nirvana/ar-turntable-history/ar-brochure.md), 1963 or later, page 8: "meets NAB specifications for broadcast equipment on wow, flutter, rumble, and speed accuracy." The same page shows the arm floating down after being dropped, and a hammer striking the top plate, with the suspension described as insensitive to mechanical shock from the floor and to acoustic feedback. Price $78 less cartridge, one-year guarantee. Page 9 carries five press excerpts; they are written out on the [period tests](period-tests.md#what-ar-quoted-from-the-press) page.

## The XB1 manual

[Installation and Operating Instructions, Model XB1](../originals/xb1-owners.pdf), p. 2, the British XB. Starred items are "guaranteed, as a condition of sale" to the same NAB standard, March 1964.

| Item | Printed |
| --- | --- |
| Speed | 33⅓ and 45 rpm ± 0.3 percent |
| Rumble | "Greater than −40 dB", as printed |
| Wow and flutter | 0.03 percent |
| Platter | 11¾ in, 4 lb |
| Guarantee | Five years |

No weighting or reference level is printed beside the rumble or the wow and flutter figure, so neither can be placed beside a figure from another table.
