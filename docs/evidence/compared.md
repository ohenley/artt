# Against the reference tables

[The evidence](README.md) · [Factory](factory.md) · [Period tests](period-tests.md) · [Owner tests](owner-tests.md) · [Where the AR is weaker](weaker.md) · [Bench method](bench-method.md)

Two numbers share a table here only if the same laboratory or the same method produced both. A maker's guaranteed limit is never set against another maker's typical figure. What cannot meet that rule goes in a list with its method named, not in a ranking. The line above each table names the lab and the method.

Before reading any wow and flutter figure, read [the misread figure](README.md#a-figure-that-is-misread-01-percent-wow-and-flutter): the XA's 0.1 percent is a limit, not a reading, and belongs in no comparison.

## A. Speed under added load: one method, one tester

Villchur, 1962, Part 2, p. 36 ([held](../originals/villchur-1962-part-2.pdf)). Label: Factory, since the designer ran the test. Starting from 2½ g of stylus force on the outer grooves, weight is added to the pickup and the speed read against the NAB limit of 0.3 percent, 21 lines a minute on a stroboscope card. The other four tables are described by type only; Villchur does not name them.

| Table | Motor and platter | 3 g added | 5 g added | Can be stopped by a finger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Number 1 | Heavy-duty hysteresis motor, very heavy platter | Inside 0.3 percent | Inside 0.3 percent | Not stated |
| Number 2 | Light motor, light platter | Inside | Inside | Yes, lightly |
| AR | Medium weight by his class | Inside | Inside | Yes, lightly |
| Number 4 | Heavy motor, heavy platter | Outside 0.3 percent | Considerably more | No, "some effort" |
| Number 5 | Lightweight | Outside 0.3 percent | Considerably more | Not stated |

This is the only held test that puts a heavy-platter table and the AR through one procedure. The heavy table with the strong motor passed; the heavy table with the weak motor failed; the AR passed. Anyone can repeat the test with a penny and a nickel: [bench method](bench-method.md#the-load-test-with-coins).

Two things these rows show without any ranking. The Technics figures, from one table, differ by 22 dB from the weighting alone. And the two *Audio* rumble figures of 1983 and 1984 cannot meet: Long's unweighted number came through the groove with the arm resonance dominating it, and the Boston Audio Society's came off the spindle with a coupler and is a spectrum, not a number. The 1983 LP12 review also lists the maker's rumble as 70 dB unweighted in its header and as 60 dB in its measured-data table; both are on the held page.

## What cannot be compared today

Wow and flutter and rumble between the XA and any reference table. The XA's only factory figures are NAB limits; the 1963 readings are raw or rms with the instrument not named; the reference tables' figures are DIN, IEC or peak-weighted typical values. Table C has an empty AR column, and the [bench method](bench-method.md) page says how to fill it.
