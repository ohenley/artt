# What independent labs and reviewers measured at the time

[The evidence](README.md) · [Factory](factory.md) · [Owner tests](owner-tests.md) · [Against the reference tables](compared.md) · [Where the AR is weaker](weaker.md) · [Bench method](bench-method.md)

Tier 2 of the evidence: measurements by people who did not make the table. Each figure carries its method as the source printed it. The three 1963 reports tested the first family; the 1984 review tested the 1983 table, and its figures are not carried to the XA.

## HiFi/Stereo Review, March 1963: Julian D. Hirsch

Technical Talk, pp. 33–34. Held: [PDF pages](../originals/hifi-stereo-review-1963-03-hirsch-ar-pp33-34.pdf). The table tested was the one-speed AR record player at $58; the two-speed model at $68 was announced as coming shortly. Label: Period.

| Item | Result | Method as printed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rumble | −38 dB with lateral and vertical components together; −40 dB with the vertical component cancelled, as when playing mono records | Against the NARTB broadcast standard of −35 dB. He adds that the low-speed motors put the rumble at about 8 cps, well below audibility, against the 30 cps rumble of 1800 rpm motors |
| Wow | 0.05 percent | "The lowest I have ever measured on a turntable" |
| Flutter | 0.02 percent | Same sentence |
| Speed | Exact. Unchanged from 80 to 140 V on the mains | No further detail |
| Warp wow | None audible with a ¼ in warp under the record | Listening |
| Tracking error | 0.4 to about 1 degree per inch, against the rated maximum of 0.32. The manufacturer answered that an error in the hand-assembled test unit caused it, and that production jigs prevent it; the magazine printed the answer in the report | Measured with the overhang set as instructed |
| Shock | The motorboard "can literally be pounded with a hammer" without a thump in the speaker or a groove jump, at 1 g tracking force | Listening |
| Acoustic feedback | Complete freedom from it; he attributes the clean sound he heard to this | Listening |
| Arm friction | Low; suitable for cartridges tracking in the 1 g range. The viscous damping "worked well" | Handling |
| Stylus-force gauge | Accurate within ¼ g | Checked by him |
| Platter | Moderately heavy, about 3 lb | Stated |

Hirsch's sentence on rumble, speed and acoustic feedback is the one AR printed in its brochure. The sentence on wow and flutter is also there. The full report shows the figures behind both.

## High Fidelity, March 1963: United States Testing Company

Equipment report, Acoustic Research Turntable and Arm, pp. 59–60. Held: [PDF pages](../originals/high-fidelity-1963-03-ar-report-pp59-60.pdf). Data for the magazine's reports came from United States Testing Company, Inc., of Hoboken, New Jersey, an independent laboratory. The table tested was the one-speed model at $58. Label: Period.

| Item | Result | Method as printed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed error | 0.05 percent at 33 rpm, "the lowest speed error USTC has encountered in turntables other than the type that employ a vernier speed adjustment" | Not further described |
| Wow | 0.08 percent rms | rms |
| Flutter | 0.04 percent rms | rms |
| Rumble | 38 dB below the reference level of 1.4 cm/sec at 100 cps. Most of it below 20 cps, the most prominent component at 17 cps | NAB reference, unweighted: see the May note below |
| Arm resonance | 17 cps, which USTC took to be the source of the 17 cps rumble component. "An arm with a lower resonance might be desirable" | Stated |
| Hum field above the platter | Negligible, among the lowest USTC had encountered | Stated |
| Start | Up to speed in less than one rotation of the platter | Stated |
| Shock and feedback | A hard vertical blow with a fist or a hammer during play does not make the stylus skip. Speakers pumping out heavy bass nearby "will have no effect" | Stated |
| Tracking | With a high-quality cartridge, tracks at forces as low as 1 g | Stated |
| Platter | Two pieces, 11¾ in overall, 3.3 lb | Stated |

**The magazine's May 1963 note on its rumble method**, p. 51. Held: [PDF page](../originals/high-fidelity-1963-05-rumble-method-p51.pdf). From January 1963 the magazine printed rumble unweighted, read directly from the test set-up "in accordance with the existing NAB specification", a 100 cps reference tone at 1.4 cm/sec peak velocity. Before then USTC had weighted its readings by the frequency of the rumble found. The AR's −38 dB is an unweighted figure; under the old weighted system it "would have been some 10 to 15 db better", in the region of −47 to −53 dB. The same note gives the Zenith Micro-Touch changer, tested in January, −37 dB by the same method.

## Audio, April 1963: equipment profile

Acoustic Research Two-Speed Turntable, pp. 48 and 54. Held: [PDF pages](../originals/audio-1963-04-ar-profile-pp48-54.pdf). The table tested was the two-speed model, $68; the writer notes the design had already been described by Villchur in the magazine's September and October 1962 issues. Label: Period.

| Item | Result | Method as printed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed | "Does run at exact speed (both speeds)" | Not described |
| Wow and flutter | Less than 0.04 percent, "and some of that is due to the measuring instrument" | Not described |
| Rumble | Down 40 dB, with the rumble frequency below 10 cps "due to the lower rpm of the motors used" | Reference level not printed |
| Tracking | Accepts cartridges that track well at 1 g; the arm's friction would allow less | Stated |
| Speed change | Lift the mat and outer platter, move the belt on the pulley; "easy, but rather lengthy for only one record" | Stated |

## What AR quoted from the press

AR's brochure, page 9 ([held](../archive/vinyl-nirvana/images/ar-brochure-2.jpg)), prints five excerpts chosen by the maker. Three of the original articles are now held, above. Two are not.

| Publication | Who | Excerpt | Original |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *HiFi/Stereo Review* | Julian Hirsch | Wow and flutter the lowest he had measured; speed exact; the only rumble audible is the record's own; clean sound from freedom from acoustic feedback | Held, above |
| *High Fidelity* | United States Testing Company | "The lowest speed error USTC has encountered in [fixed speed] turntables", no hint of rumble | Held, above |
| *Audio* | Not named | Exact speed at both speeds; as little signal of its own as any table the magazine had tested | Held, above |
| *Modern Hi-Fi* | John Milder | The best answer so far to rumble and acoustic feedback. No feedback even with the table placed directly on top of a wide-range speaker system | Not held: SR40 in [sources to read](../sources-to-read.md) |
| *Industrial Design* | Not named | "Noteworthy for elegant simplicity" | Not held |

The same page states that in 1963 *Gentlemen's Quarterly* chose the AR turntable for its top system, at $3,824; *Popular Science* for its medium-cost and luxury systems; and *Bravo* for all three of its systems. None of those articles is held: SR41.

Milder's test, the table on top of the playing speaker, is the one repeated on film in 2018 with the same result: see [owner tests](owner-tests.md).

## The 1983 table: Audio, July 1984

Peter W. Mitchell, equipment profile, The AR Turntable. Held: [archived copy](../archive/gamma-electronics/audio-1984-07-ar-turntable.md). Measurements by Alvin Foster and J. K. Pollard of the Boston Audio Society. This is the second-family table of 1983 with its Japanese-made arm. Nothing in this table applies to the XA, XB or AR77XB. Label: Period.

| Item | Result | Method as printed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed | Exactly correct at 33⅓ and 45 rpm. Unaffected by mains voltage from 75 to 130 V | Not further described |
| Speed under load | Dropped 0.27 percent under a 10 g load. A Discwasher brush stops the platter | Load on the pickup |
| Wow and flutter | 0.05 percent, against 0.04 percent claimed | DIN weighted |
| Rumble | A narrow peak of −40 dB at 6 Hz, −60 dB at 12 Hz, −70 dB at 20 Hz, −80 dB at 30 Hz, −90 dB at audible frequencies. "Less rumble than any other turntable we have measured to date" | Unweighted spectrum, measured at the spindle with the Thorens Rumpelmesskoppler, which excludes the test record's own rumble |
| Acoustic feedback | 19 dB more system gain before a low-frequency howl than a Kenwood KD-500 direct drive with an SME Series III Improved arm | Both tables 1 m from a full-range speaker on the same bench, gain raised until howl |
| Suspension | Resonance about 3 Hz. Needs a shelf that transmits no lateral or tipping motion | Stated |
| Platters | Each ring rings like a bell alone; assembled, "quite amazingly, dead", even without a mat | Tap test |
| Arm resonance | 7.5 Hz with a Shure V15 Type IV, peaking at only 8 dB; undamped low-friction arms typically exceed 20 dB. With the cartridge's brush down, a 2 dB rise at 11 to 16 Hz | Same cartridge as in the magazine's earlier arm tests |
| Tracking-force variation | 0.2 g peak to peak on a flat record, 0.5 g on a 4 mm warp | Strain-gauge cartridge |
| Arm pivot height | Nearly ½ in above the record surface; "some warp wow may be audible" | Stated |
| Microphonics | About average; about 6 dB better with a Platter Matter mat, which nearly bottomed the suspension | Stylus in a stationary groove, midrange white noise played loud, cartridge output measured |
| Cable capacitance | 82 pF per channel, against 85 pF claimed | Measured |
| Tracking-force dial | Accurate within 0.1 g | Measured |

The same review gives the first family's history as the writer knew it: introduced in 1962 at $58, more than a third of a million sold in 17 years, and a list of its faults, written out on the [weaknesses](weaker.md) page.
