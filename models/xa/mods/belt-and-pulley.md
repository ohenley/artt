# Belt and machined pulley

[XA](../README.md) · [Mods](README.md) · [Home](../../../README.md)

## Purpose

Restore speed accuracy and clean starts with a new belt. A machined pulley is a further option, but only for some motors.

The listed belt also names the XB and XB77. Check the pulley against your motor on any model.

## Stock situation

See [Motor identification](../README.md#motor-identification). Pulleys differ by motor and mains frequency, and original belt dimensions are not established.

## Procedure

1. Read the name on the motor, usually on its underside.
2. Clean the pulley, and fit the new belt around the inner platter and pulley.
3. If fitting a new pulley, pull the old one straight off. The manual suggests prying gently with two large spoons. Press the new one straight on.
4. Run the table for a week before judging. Vinyl Nirvana says its belt takes about that long to wear in.

## Gotchas

- **The machined pulley does not fit stock motors.** The maker says it fits Hurst motors only. Original Haydon and Philips XAs cannot use it without a motor change.
- **Pulleys are not interchangeable.** Philips and Haydon bores differ, at ¹⁄₁₆ in and ⅛ in, and 50 Hz pulleys are larger.
- **Belts need talc.** Galo says to use pure talc, not talcum powder, if a new belt comes uncoated.
- **A tight belt pulls the suspension.** Vinyl Nirvana warns that an overly tight belt drags the subchassis toward the motor.
- **The old belt source may have changed.** Galo's supplier is from 2008, so check that it still trades.

## Verification

1. Check speed with a strobe disc. The XA manual allows no more than ±21 dots per minute.
2. The belt runs centered on the pulley. XB note 12 sets this with shims under the motor.
3. The table starts cleanly, with no chatter.
