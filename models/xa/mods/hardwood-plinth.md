# Hardwood plinth

[XA](../README.md) · [Mods](README.md) · [Home](../../../README.md)

## Purpose

Replace a damaged or low-grade base with solid hardwood. Vinyl Nirvana says early XA bases had solid walnut sides, then walnut veneer, then a walnut-look vinyl. Chipboard is reported for the XB and AR77XB, so the mod applies to them too.

Building all three plinth mods at once: see the [plinth rebuild](plinth-rebuild.md).

## Procedure

1. Measure your existing base: outside size, wall thickness, top rebate, corner braces, and every fixing position.
2. Copy that profile. Mike Beauchamp copied his XB's profile exactly, including the slight angle at the top, then mitred long extruded lengths.
3. Check the depth under the top plate against your springs and studs.
4. Plan the cutouts first, if you are also fitting [RCA jacks](rca-jacks.md) or an [IEC inlet](iec-inlet.md).
5. Test-fit the top plate. On the XB, note 16 of drawing 119 wants it flush at the front of the cabinet, to leave the arm least interference with the dust cover.

## Gotchas

- **Solid hardwood moves with humidity.** Use quartersawn stock or a laminated build, and keep the top plate mounting points independent of seasonal movement.
- **Plinth weight does not change the suspension tuning.** The suspension is tuned to the subchassis mass. Feet only need to be stable, not soft.
- **Longer studs may not fit.** One Mayware arm build left heavier springs and studs unused because they were too long for the stock plinth. Check depth if you plan a [suspension kit](suspension-kit.md).
- **Your dust cover has to fit too.** A new outer size can stop a cover from seating.

## Verification

1. The top plate sits flat with no rocking, and the subchassis moves freely in every direction.
2. Set the platter clearance: see [Service details](../README.md#service-details).
3. The dust cover seats properly, and clears the arm at rest.
4. Check the fit again after a change of season.
